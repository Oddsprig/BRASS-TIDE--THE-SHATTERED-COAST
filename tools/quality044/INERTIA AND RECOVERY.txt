ONE TUNING POINT
motion_with_status now divides the existing velocity response by one inertia
factor: clamp(hull size / 14, 1, 2.5). This same response controls acceleration,
braking and retention of the old velocity during a course change. There is no
new hull data, saved momentum state, public API, cooldown or separate system.
Tinwake's size 14 retains the exact previous response. Smaller or missing-size
hulls also retain it. Size 21 Ashwake uses 1.5 times that response duration;
size 30 Furnace uses about 2.14 times. The cap is 2.5 times for size 35+.
These are authored parameter ratios, NOT measured stopping or turn results.

The desired velocity, top-speed target, existing angle/turn-rate limit,
throttle and boost multipliers are unchanged. Engine/crew/flood status and
empty-fuel factors still enter through the existing code. Propulsion fuel
debit and invalid-equipment handling are unchanged. Damage.tick_ship still
owns crew allocation, bailing, patching, machinery work and finite supplies.
Heavier boats can coast farther while braking or turning; no instant velocity
rotation or extra force has been added. Light boats retain their escape tool.

ONE FOCUSED FIXTURE PREPARED, NOT RUN
test_combat_pressure044.gd uses actual world hulls, stock fitting, canonical
EquipmentRecords initialization and public Damage projections. No Main scene,
planner loop, combat shots, rendering, audio, saves or owner profiles are exercised.
The fixture has bounded loops at 30Hz and emits the observed numerical metrics:
  - Tin/Ash/Furnace stopping time to <=0.5 units/s and distance at their actual
    cruising speeds, plus equal-60-speed Tin/Furnace trials to isolate inertia.
  - Speed fraction after one/three seconds; 90-degree bow and velocity-course
    alignment times; course lag, forward travel and maximum angular step.
  - Actual Battle._move versus Certification.motion and direct shared-helper
    parity through boost, turn, fuel exhaustion, canonical engine damage and
    braking. Forecast calls must leave their source boat/status unchanged.
  - Two ten-second withdrawals: dry Tin versus an otherwise matching Tin with
    25% water, area-90 breach and 0.02 carried kits. Both start outbound at the
    same speed, at Tin's real firing range from identical pursuing Ash boats.
    Damage.tick_ship and Battle._move advance all four boats. This intentionally
    isolates withdrawal mechanics; no firing, hits, role decisions or tactical
    certification success are inferred. The escape boundary is Ash's real range.
  - The actual late-withdrawal state with a damaged installed engine is copied
    into paired recovery cases carrying three versus zero kits from the start.
    Six seconds on bail policy, then 24 on repair use real crew and Damage work.
    The kit ledger must match actual debits, no-kit bailing cannot patch or
    restore machinery, repair respects the existing jury cap, and crew/ammo
    cannot appear. A separate one-second return leg starts the recovered boat
    from rest and must consume propulsion fuel. That reset is a fixture boundary,
    not a claim that recovery or throttle input instantly stops a live boat.

Assertions compare measured outcomes and resource/trajectory consistency;
there is no copied exponential response or computed expected stopping formula.
Broad agility/momentum bounds are prospective acceptance criteria, not passing
evidence. The fixture's worst-case loops are finite; wall time is UNMEASURED.
It is one fixture for Root's existing bounded runner, not a new test framework.

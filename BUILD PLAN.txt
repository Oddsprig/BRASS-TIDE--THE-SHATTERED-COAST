DECISIONS

4. Use recorded weapon transients and material impacts. Music stays on a separate bus and does not contain gunfire or baked-in sea effects.
5. Expand the current 2,600 x 1,800 world toward 10,400 x 7,200 units: four times each dimension and sixteen times the area. Keep the ten existing port identities first; author wider channels, distinct coastal regions and larger dual-port coastlines. Do not just add blank border water.
6. Start the player in a readable regional view. A whole-territory overview is a navigation tool, not the default tiny-village display.

BASELINE FINDINGS — v0.002 SCREENSHOTS AND SOURCE (historical)
BoatArt._one_hull (game/scripts/boat_art.gd) draws a complete dark outline polygon at height -7 and another full outline at -2 before drawing the hull at positive heights. The projection moves those negative-height copies down the screen. This explains the detached hull/reflection appearance; it is not a fluid effect. Simply adding more planks or rivets will not repair that construction.

The current combat effects in Chart.draw_battle are mostly expanding circles/arcs. Muzzle, splash and explosion effects share this treatment. Main._physics_process only detects flagship ammunition changes for shot sound. Sound._ready makes one generic synthetic shot, one boom and a bell. Enemy fire and allied secondary boats therefore lack individual firing feedback. This needs an event connection as well as better samples.

The world is 2,600 x 1,800. Current nearest-port separations are about 260–517 units; the port bounds span only 1,730 by 890 units. Chart zoom-out and always-visible names/industry labels compound the crowding. Camera/grid limits and campaign navigation still contain fixed old-world dimensions.

A title_screen and Escape captain's desk already exist, but the requested work is a clearer complete menu flow and an actual configurable fleet test mode. The current Quick battle is one preset, not a battle builder.

v0.004 — COMBAT THAT READS AND SOUNDS LIKE MOTORBOAT COMBAT
Visual language:
- Machine guns: sharp short muzzle flash at the correct barrel, interrupted tracer streaks, small material hits and short missed-shot water kicks. A rapid gun must look automatic. If its cycle rate rises, rebalance per-round damage and paid ammunition use together; never show imaginary shots while retaining a fake ammo count.
- Cannons: brief bright flash, visible barrel recoil/return, a heavier shell streak, local smoke, splinter/plate fragments and a separate water impact for misses.
- Rockets: rack ignition, short exhaust, capped smoke trail and a compact impact/fire effect. Avoid giant glowing circles covering both hulls.
- Torpedoes: readable moving body and thin foam trail at water height; water-column impact, hull shock and persistent breach/flooding consequences. No laser appearance or constant fireball.
- Distinguish wood hits, armor hits, engine damage, fire and sinking. Sink in a short controlled hull-down/foam/wreck sequence rather than disappearing behind a generic circle.
- Keep selected outlines, orders and health bars readable through effects. Camera shake is restrained, optional and can be zero.

Audio connection:
Battle emits bounded typed events for fire, hit, splash, engine failure and sinking, including source/target IDs, mount, weapon family, position, material and event sequence. Main consumes each event once. Sound playback must not depend on flagship ammo comparisons. All player and enemy boats contribute.

Audio bank and mix:
Recorded gunshot attack + body + appropriate short outdoor tail, mechanical action and material-specific impact. Separate light/heavy gun and cannon variants, rocket ignition/flight/impact, torpedo launch/water impact, engine idle/load/damage, hull creaks, fire and bailing/pumps. No procedural sine-tone ping as weapon or impact feedback. Use variation without immediate repeats, restrained pitch variation, camera-aware panning/distance, priority and per-category voice limits. Start with a 32-voice budget and measure it; near relevant attacks and major impacts take priority over distant detail. Automatic fire uses correctly timed single shots or authored start/body/tail segments; do not restart a long burst every frame. Music ducks modestly during important combat feedback. Headroom and a limiter prevent a forty-boat battle becoming clipped noise.

Sources are researched in WEAPON SOUND RESEARCH.txt. Select and audition a small first bank; verify individual rights, keep acquisition evidence and produce proper credits. Paid libraries are options, not purchases made or required by this plan. Do not rip sounds from another game or a random video. Source archives and extracted licensed WAVs stay in a local audio work folder, not a public Git source ZIP; commit a manifest/import recipe and distribute only what the license allows inside the finished game.

Exit gate:
One representative firing/hit/miss/sinking sequence for each family is unmistakable at play zoom. A forty-boat scenario emits audio for both teams, respects caps and does not reuse a bell as a gun. Audio source/credit audit passes. Numerical samples cannot decide whether it sounds good: export short audition files for the owner to hear voluntarily, while automated runs stay silent/background.

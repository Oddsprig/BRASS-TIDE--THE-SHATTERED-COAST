BRASS TIDE — AI BEHAVIOR STANDARD v0.029-v0.038
23 October 2026 | OWNER REQUIREMENT | PLANNED, NOT IMPLEMENTED

QUALITY TARGET
Owner requires at least the depth and polish they admire in Starsector, across
both campaign fleets and individual battle boats. Light craft must be able to
encircle and harass, and pirates/opposing factions must fight independently.
This is the design and acceptance target. Current v0.028 does not meet it yet;
passing numerical tests alone cannot establish comparable quality or enjoyment.
Use original behavior/content. No proprietary algorithm or asset is copied.

CURRENT FOUNDATION AND GAPS
v0.028 already has moving traders/patrols/raiders, nearby NPC-NPC encounter
resolution, persistent paid recovery/replacement, real stores and hostile faction
checks. Battle boats lead shots, respect actual mount arcs/ranges, choose simple
engagement distance, follow orders and avoid land. Current strategic knowledge
is too global and tactical target choice is too heavily nearest-enemy based.
Future work must strengthen these existing systems, not claim their basic debut.

A. CAMPAIGN ACTORS — A WORLD THAT OPERATES WITHOUT THE PLAYER
Every active owner has an explained objective, actual boats, crew, cargo/stores,
purse, observed intelligence, home/support access and a route/recovery plan.
Lifecycle: observe -> evaluate -> choose/continue objective -> sail -> intercept,
escort, avoid or join a real encounter -> disengage/settle -> repair/resupply,
recruit or commission a replacement -> return to duty when actually ready.

Role decisions:
- Pirates value capturable cargo and vulnerable targets against expected escort
  strength, speed/escape options, own damage/ammunition and cost of returning.
  A laden merchant is attractive; a powerful escort can deter or defeat a raid.
  Pirates can flee, regroup and seek another observed target instead of always
  charging. No universal hostility toward every actor or permanent player chase.
- Patrols protect approaches and shipments, react to credible recent sightings,
  pursue hostile raiders within sustainable reach and return when protection or
  stores demand it. A pursuit does not abandon every other obligation forever.
- Conflict factions independently raid, intercept, defend and escort according
  to their actual pairwise relations, military objective and supplies. A hostile
  faction can attack another faction without the player being nearby or involved.
- Traders/fishers/tankers/yachts have their own jobs and risk tolerance. Evade,
  wait for escorts, return home or choose a safer route when the evidence and
  actual capabilities support it. Ordinary trade continues during a quiet coast.
- Allied, neutral and hostile actors remain distinct. Encounter joining checks
  relationship to each side, observed fight, actual distance/time and available
  force. Neutrals do not become hostile through proximity or because the player
  looked at them. Ceasefires/peace alter future targeting through existing law.
- Three-way conflict preserves a hostility graph: pirates and two mutually
  hostile factions are not one enemy team just because each opposes the player.
  Alliances/neutrality are checked per participant on joining and on deliberate
  hostile acts. Attribute damaging acts, surrender, capture and prize rights to
  their actual actors. Do not grant another party's prize or recruit neutrals
  automatically. Extend the current two-team assumptions where needed; do not
  claim a three-party test passed by merging mutually hostile forces.
- Allied coalition allocations respect the normal 300 DP shared deployment limit;
  genuinely independent hostile sides have their own cap. Declare supported
  active-party/hull/objective counts before testing, including reserve/wave
  rules. A neutral objective is not an unlimited free armed coalition.

Information and pursuit:
- Decisions use personal/faction-shared dated observations and source provenance,
  not all global live fleet positions. Define how reports are obtained/shared;
  a shared report is not an omniscient feed. Apply the same rules to NPC targets.
  Define separate evidence for identity, fitting, cargo value, condition and
  intention; seeing a hull does not reveal its entire manifest. First observed
  weapons update knowledge explicitly; uncertainty grows with age and distance.
- A lost contact remains a last-seen course/area with age. Search, investigate,
  reacquire or abandon; do not keep following exact unseen coordinates.
- Compare own and observed enemy force, fittings, stores, speed, terrain and
  objective value with uncertainty. Re-evaluate when evidence changes, using
  commitment windows/hysteresis so fleets do not flip jobs every update.
- Interception and reinforcement require actual movement. Show why a fleet is
  pursuing, escorting, waiting or returning. Breaking pursuit takes real escape,
  obscuration or a defeated pursuer; no teleport or invulnerable cooldown trick.

Independent conflict and aftermath:
- Pirates can raid traders/fishers/tankers/yachts; patrols and escorts can fight
  pirates; mutually hostile factions fight each other. These interactions must
  occur while the player stays docked elsewhere and must change real conditions.
- Stable encounter IDs and participant hull UIDs own one active resolution.
  Prevent overlapping pair fights, duplicate joins, repeated rewards, restored
  casualties or ships simultaneously sailing and fighting in two places.
  Reserve each ship UID exclusively at encounter entry; persist orders, timers,
  pending launch/ammo commitments and joining/withdrawal/surrender transitions.
  Release that reservation at the defined physical exit/settlement. Once-only
  final rewards alone do not prevent two simultaneous fights using the same ship.
- Survivors keep their actual crew, wounds, breaches, engine/mount condition,
  cargo, working ammunition/fuel and losses. Wrecks and captured stock come from
  those same hulls. Trade interruption affects actual supply and construction.
- Repair, recruitment and replacement require a reachable working harbor, real
  materials/workers, payment and elapsed work. An insolvent/destroyed actor can
  fail to recover. No nightly purse reset, free escort spawn or revenge fleet.
- Save/load, player approach/departure and changes of simulation detail must
  preserve the encounter and ledger; none reroll the outcome or heal a fleet.

B. BATTLE ROLES — COORDINATED BOATS, NOT A SHARED CHARGE
Use a group intent layer, role/target selection, reachable station planning,
short-horizon helm decisions and independent physical weapon control. These are
logical responsibilities, not permission to replace the existing battle engine.
Both AI sides and the autopilot use the same rules and physical constraints.

Tactical roles:
- Light skirmisher: probe an exposed approach, threaten a useful target, make a
  firing pass, peel away under danger and reform. Harassment can distract/turn a
  heavier opponent or open a firing opportunity for another boat.
- Torpedo striker: build an actual launch solution and attack window, coordinate
  with allied pressure, spend finite ammunition, then turn away and reposition.
  A fixed tube needs hull alignment; no sideways torpedo launch or free reload.
- Gunboat/artillery: choose a lane and range for its useful working mounts,
  avoid being isolated and respond to flankers by turning, repositioning or
  relying on an actual escort. It is allowed to counter and defeat light boats.
- Screen/escort: protect a named vulnerable ally, occupy threat approaches and
  intercept attackers without abandoning the protected boat for a distant kill.
- Civilian/support: seek the objective/exit, preserve cargo and people, maintain
  useful escort separation and seek shelter. A yacht or tanker must not inherit
  a gunboat's compulsory close-combat charge simply because it has a small gun.

Encirclement and harassment requirements:
1. Two or more suitable light boats can allocate different reachable sectors
   around a target, based on observed weapon arcs, target motion, obstacles and
   allied positions. Avoid stacking every boat in the same approach lane.
2. Choose a useful firing window or threat position, not a cosmetic orbit.
   Keep own actual guns able to bear; exploit weaker coverage when observed.
   An angle that only looks like a flank but never permits a shot is a failure.
3. Use physical speed, acceleration, turning and collision footprints. Circling
   is an available maneuver, not a command to spin forever. Narrow passages,
   an agile target, a screen or insufficient speed can make encirclement unwise.
4. Vary approach, pressure and breakaway. Under concentrated fire, dangerous
   flooding, weapon loss or an unsuitable matchup, peel toward an escape lane,
   use an actual screen/cover and regroup or withdraw as doctrine permits.
5. Keep spacing, avoid trapping allies against land and deconflict torpedo
   approaches. Reassign a lost/blocked station and a destroyed leader. There
   is no invisible formation damage/defense bonus for occupying a sector.
6. Harass for a purpose: threaten cargo, draw aim, pressure a weak side, support
   a strike or delay pursuit. Do not require frigate-like boats to beat a heavier
   ship in every test; target counters and meaningful failure must remain viable.

Selection, survival and command:
- Score targets by mission threat/value, role suitability, observed vulnerability,
  firing access, friendly commitments and escape cost. Nearest is one input.
- Use real fitted ranges/arcs, ammunition, crew, flood/fire and machinery state.
  Avoid wasteful rockets/torpedoes at hopeless or blocked solutions. State clearly
  whether a weapon is withholding, aligning, reloading, disabled or out of stock.
- Define cautious, steady and aggressive choices through positioning, risk and
  withdrawal thresholds; no hidden damage or resource bonuses. Profiles may
  change priorities, not turn an impossible maneuver into possible movement.
- Explicit orders/manual override stay authoritative and visibly acknowledged.
  Automatic survival decisions explain themselves; any impossible order states
  its limitation. Control switches do not silently erase player command intent.
- Define explicit order lifetime, completion/cancellation and return to doctrine;
  escort separation and pursuit/harassment time/risk limits prevent abandonment
  or endless jobs. Every chosen sector needs an executable approach and exit
  accounting for turning radius, moving allies and occupied berths.
- Use stable targets/stations with hysteresis, interrupted by meaningful danger,
  lost targets or explicit orders. Avoid jitter, endless circling, stuck chasing
  a sunk/prized hull, runaway reversal and formation deadlock.

C. CIVILIANS, DEPLOYMENT AND FAIR RESOLUTION
Interception must endanger the actual cargo/passenger/fuel carriers. Designating
an owned boat support-only cannot make it or its cargo immune to piracy.
- Threatened unarmed carriers appear as physical objective boats in bounded
  mission slots with real exit routes. They provide no free combat/support buff.
- Armed commercial conversions use normal deployment cost within 300 points.
  Never exempt a heavily armed transport by calling it a civilian objective.
- Define how overflow carriers/reserves enter, escape or remain at risk; do not
  teleport their manifests home, duplicate hulls into waves or hide unlimited
  free guns. Stored boats at another port are not magically present in a convoy.
- Both sides can target relevant carriers according to the mission/hostility.
  Carrier capture, sinking and escape settle each physical manifest exactly once.

Distant battles need an inexpensive resolution mode. It must use the same real
hull/fitting/condition/crew/store, hostility, deployment and custody conventions.
Include calibrated role/doctrine/escort effects, escape and damage rather than a
raw hull-count victory roll. Compare repeated seeded matchup outcomes with normal
tactical fixtures and set acceptable error bands before tuning. Different detail
levels need not produce identical trajectories, but player presence must not
systematically reverse winners or grant free stores/repairs. Predeclare per-
matchup error metrics for survival/capture, hull damage, crew, ammunition,
manifest and operating cost, with held-out fixtures separated by role and side.
Aggregate win rate alone is insufficient. Promote/demote one
canonical encounter; do not run both resolvers on the same hulls simultaneously.

D. PERFORMANCE AND EVIDENCE
Bound decision budgets, contact memory, group stations, encounter records and
spatial queries. Stagger strategic choices and sample tactical intent at a
profiled cadence while steering/projectiles continue smoothly. Do not solve all
world paths every frame. Pure workers use copied data, join before main-thread
canonical publication and never manipulate scene/UI objects. Keep Separate
rendering, cheap water, numerical crews and serialized hidden/silent tests.

Required scenario matrix, in both team orientations and varied seeds:
A1 Three light boats versus a heavy: separated reachable sectors, useful firing
   passes and breakaways; no guaranteed light-boat victory requirement.
A2 Same heavy with a screen: coordinated harassment meets a credible counter.
A3 Fixed-tube strike: legal launch alignment, sensible ammunition use, turn-away;
   test blocked shot, spent tubes, fast/slow targets and an escaping carrier.
A4 Focused/damaged skirmisher: prioritises escape/repair appropriately, can lose
   if trapped, rejoins only when conditions permit; no instant condition reset.
A5 Island channel/destroyed leader: choose a feasible substitute station or
   abandon encirclement; no land penetration, deadlock or endless pursuit.
A6 Player docked elsewhere: real pirate -> loaded convoy -> responding patrol,
   plus an independently hostile faction encounter. Peace/neutral controls must
   not fight. Survivors spend actual resources and may fail to recover.
A7 Coalition/overlapping contacts: a nearby ally joins once, a neutral stays out,
   deployed limits hold and each UID has one encounter/aftermath at a time.
A8 Distant/near transition and mid-encounter save: no duplicated losses/loot,
   healed boats, rerolled rewards or reset pursuit. Compare calibrated outcomes.
A9 Civilian convoy with armed/unarmed carriers and support/reserves: actual
   cargo/fuel/passengers are capturable/lost/escaped, never blanket invulnerable.

Before implementation/tuning, record supported active fleet/party/carrier counts,
strategic/tactical decision-latency targets and real-time CPU/frame budgets in
a versioned fixture manifest. Report overruns, stalls and dropped decisions,
not only average time. Use held-out seeds and adverse matchups for acceptance.
A7 must include a merchant convoy, mutually hostile pirates and faction raiders,
and neutral fishing traffic, with blocked-flank and depleted-ammunition variants.
A8 must interrupt entry/wave release/withdrawal/capture with reinforcements still
travelling; all reservations, state and physical arrival/exit times persist.

Record intention, chosen role/target/station, firing opportunities, separation,
withdrawal causes, damage/stores and objective outcomes. Freeze scenario criteria
before tuning, including counterexamples, so cosmetic movement cannot pass.
Review motion sequences and battle-context captures: circles that never attack,
collisions, confusion and unresponsive orders are failures even if a test exits0.
Voluntary comparative play/listening can establish subjective quality; do not
invent such evidence or use headless timings as rendered FPS. Track every unmet
behavior visibly in acceptance notes. No unsupported claim of quality parity.

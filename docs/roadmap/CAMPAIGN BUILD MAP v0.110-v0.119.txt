**The story bible**

A delayed merchant convoy exposes something worse than ordinary raiding: paid escorts have been abandoning boats whose owners refuse restrictive rescue agreements.

**Della Venn**, a Compact charter administrator, once kept vulnerable harbors supplied through genuine disasters. She now converts rescue debts into control of passage rights. Her protection works for favored clients, which makes her dangerous: simply destroying her wardens could strand the people the player wants to help.

The player remains an independent captain. Participation is optional, and neither faction allegiance nor completion of the old eight missions is required.

The following histories are **new writing proposals**, not claims about existing lore:

- **Tobin Hale**, at Brass Haven, financed some early guarantees. He initially defends Venn because he remembers the boats her organization saved. His growth comes through admitting his responsibility and risking actual escrow, rather than offering another speech.
- **Mara Voss**, at Anvil Dock, depends on reliable orders to keep shipwrights employed. She opposes coercion but refuses to pretend that repairs, materials and skilled labor become free under a kinder banner.
- **Sister Wren**, at Reedwater Floats, wants public accountability. Experience makes her confront the practical consequences of exposing witnesses before there are boats and safe berths to protect them.
- **Cass Rye**, at Rust Anchorage, knows how impoundment and resale work. She supplied a route used in an earlier seizure. Her useful knowledge and selective honesty make cooperation uncomfortable.
- **Venn** appears early as an effective organizer, offers credible bargains, and acts against the replacement effort. She is not introduced only at the final reveal.

Use one existing native captain as a recurring warden or rival where identity and survival permit. Bind the actual owner ID; a dead captain stays dead. Crew reactions refer to the player’s surviving complement and actual service history.

The four acts are:

1. **The guarantee:** rescue, seizure and evacuation reveal who receives protection.
2. **The exception list:** Venn’s useful work complicates the accusation; witnesses force a commitment.
3. **The replacement:** the player must secure ships, fuel, labor and support while Venn interferes.
4. **The passage:** the proposed alternative must survive a real voyage, followed by a personal harbor reckoning.

**Two commitments with physical consequences**

**C1 — Public disclosure or protected testimony.** Public disclosure exposes named crews and requires moving and protecting their actual cohort at Reedwater Floats; Venn receives warning. Protected testimony keeps identities restricted and requires safe passage and reception at Anvil Dock under the existing service rules. The choice changes the people, destination and protection obligation—not merely prices or reputation.

**C2 — Mutual rescue pool or bonded owner service.** The mutual arrangement pools funded escorts and repair obligations. The bonded arrangement purchases defined service and priority through separate owners. Both require real sponsors, stock and labor. Neither grants ports, sovereignty, free fleets or worldwide peace.

**Three endings**

- **Common Passage:** the mutual arrangement funds and completes its first relief passage. Contact scenes acknowledge who paid, who survived and who remains excluded.
- **Many Harbors:** the bonded arrangement completes its passage under explicit obligations and limits. It is a viable political compromise, with different beneficiaries.
- **Unsettled Coast:** the replacement fails or the player explicitly closes the effort after withdrawal. Native losses remain; surviving people and unresolved coercion are acknowledged.

**Consent is stored separately from outcome.** Retreat never means accepting Venn’s terms. Any negotiated concession requires an explicit choice. A retreat can remain resumable; partial closure happens at debrief, not automatically during battle.

**Decisive minimal integration recommendation**

Use **one main coastal-story chain plus a narrow `arc110` adapter**. The existing story system owns presentation and progression; the adapter binds native operations to their outcomes. It is not another mission engine.

For v110:

1. Add the opening chain/content to `game/data/coastal_stories.json`. Preserve every existing chain, stage and choice ID. Establish stable new operation IDs and valid non-advancing boundaries for later releases.
2. Reuse `StoryOps.start/choose/refresh`, the existing offers and story UI.
3. Launch the opening through the actual `Campaign.accept_voyage_job(id, carrier_uid)` path. Select and bind an eligible native convoy; retain its job, owner and carrier identities. Do not synthesize a completed escort.
4. In `Campaign.settle_voyage_job`, capture the job before `VoyageJobs.finish` closes it. After successful native settlement, immediately record the matching operation outcome. Do not rely solely on `lastEscortVoyage`, which another completed escort can replace.
5. Keep at most one pending main operation and twelve bounded outcome records. Validate them in `campaign_state_validation.gd`. Do not place twelve outcomes into the eight-entry `usedOffers`.
6. Add explicit recognized objective gating before exposing choices. Unknown gate types currently pass, so a new gate name alone provides no protection.

For later battle operations, finish native cargo, casualty and payment settlement first. Attach operation evidence before caching `physical_battle.settlementReport`; an idempotent consumer handles both initial and cached reports. Require the bound operation and physical encounter identity. Empty-physical legacy “won” calls cannot prove a campaign operation.

The supplied `VoyageJobs.finish` already checks loaded escort arrival, unresolved interception, destination, lost freight, exact escrow and actual charter discharge. Reuse those checks. **Legacy `Contracts` has one active contract slot; do not overwrite it. VoyageJobs is a separate collection—do not impose an invented global one-job restriction.**

Never set new work’s `contract.story=true`: that advances the old mission index. Likewise, preserve legacy `completed`; the new arc uses its own completion. Root’s later Main inspection found no demonstrated hard world ending, so no speculative Main rewrite is included.

**Independent red-team findings incorporated**

The reviewer returned six initial risks and three specific objections to the synthesized draft:

- **First-voyage bankruptcy:** mandatory supplies or repairs could strand progression. Opening acceptance must show a funded outward-and-return route without unique salvage or a hull purchase.
- **Old-save damage:** reserved cargo and existing story state must survive. New operation bindings are namespaced and released stage IDs remain valid.
- **Cosmetic C1:** public/private wording repeated existing chains. C1 now changes the actual witness destination and protection duty.
- **Dead former enemy:** a required captain might already be dead. Actor-dependent content now has a distinct native alternative; optional Cass work does not duplicate the main operation.
- **False consent:** “Guarantee Remains” wrongly combined retreat with agreement. The third ending is now **Unsettled Coast**, with consent recorded separately.
- **Repetition, unseen expiry and invented fleets:** operations need distinct protection, seizure, evacuation and withdrawal decisions; narrative progress must not silently expire; every encounter uses finite funded assets.

These are design findings, **not runtime certification**. The targeted red-team follow-up is still finishing.

ROOT INTEGRATION CLARIFICATION — v110
The opening uses a loaded merchant escort, not a tow. Existing fresh-career merchants leave Kelp Reach and Anvil Dock; none is assumed to be beside Brass Haven. Tobin provides a dated departure lead, then the captain approaches an actually sighted client. No live unseen cargo is revealed. Escort acceptance uses VoyageJobs.accept_escort, not the harbor-only accept method. A bounded player follow order may assist the existing escort; it does not pause, reroute, stock or fund its NPC. A missing or lost client means another eligible real passage, never a respawn. Content boundaries remain valid saved stages in each intermediate release.

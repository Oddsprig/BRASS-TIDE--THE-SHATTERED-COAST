BRASS TIDE - OCEAN WORLD AND FACTION DESIGN
29 October 2026 | Owner clarification | Required campaign direction

WORLD STRUCTURE
The setting is one ocean-covered planet with islands and groups of islands.
There are no major continents. This is a large campaign world whose geography,
politics, shipping and exploration persist between visits.

Use these gameplay roles consistently:
- Open seas: the hyperspace role. A large playable travel layer connecting
  archipelagos, with shipping, pursuit, piracy, sightings and route choices.
- Island group / archipelago: the star-system role. Enter its local coastal
  chart to navigate islands, visit harbors, explore and meet nearby fleets.
- Individual island: the planet role. Its resources, settlements, industry,
  hazards, exploration opportunities and political control make it distinct.
- Harbor, outpost or facility: a market or installation on an island. Its
  actual workforce, inventory, productive equipment and purses remain finite.
- Sector: a larger geographic part of the ocean, containing several groups.
  Sector identity is distinct from a faction's changing territorial influence.
  Sectors are regions/overlays on the open-seas chart, not another travel layer.

This is a sea-world analogy, not literal space travel. Open seas remain water;
the existing boats, cargo, crews, fuel and clock continue across both layers.
An island may have several harbors or installations. Its strategic role must
not collapse into a destination dot with one generic menu.

GENERATED CAMPAIGNS
The sector-generation step must create a persistent campaign from a seed:
archipelago placement, useful navigable connections, island layouts/resources,
settlements and starting faction ownership. Blend authored important locations
and stories with procedural variation. The current 48-group target is breadth
to prove, not a substitute for useful destinations or a delivered generator.
Preserve the starting coast and existing voyages when adding world generation;
old saves must not silently regenerate their islands, ownership or inventories.
Visited regions remain the same on return. Unvisited regions still have stable
identities and a saved simulation history; loading one does not reroll its wealth.

POLITICAL OWNERSHIP AND THE MAP
An archipelago is a geographic container, not an exclusive faction container.
Different factions may own different islands in the same group. Different
harbors on one island may also be controlled by different factions.
The outer map must distinguish a single-owner group, mixed peaceful ownership,
allied/shared administration, contested territory, neutral/unclaimed and unknown.
Show known ownership with faction emblems/segments and a readable summary;
the local chart shows which actual islands, harbors and outposts support it.
Do not reveal unsighted bases or private political intelligence through an overlay.

Ownership, claims, access rights, military presence and influence are distinct.
Sailing a patrol through a group does not transfer ownership of its islands.
Shared control of a GROUP naturally follows different factions holding assets
inside it. Actual joint administration of one harbor needs an explicit agreement
about control, access, revenue and defense, with one inventory and workforce.
Private businesses and shipping-company assets are separate from sovereignty.

FACTIONS AND A CHANGING WORLD
Use multiple political and commercial actors: trading powers, shipping firms,
industrial or fishing interests, independent settlements, patrols and pirates.
Commercial alignment, player reputation and faction-to-faction relationships
affect access, inspections, prices, commissions, protection and hostile behavior.
Fleets must act on those relationships and pursue real campaign objectives.
Merchants ship actual goods; escorts protect them; raiders intercept them;
patrols respond, and hostile factions can fight without the player present.

Faction expansion is a required later campaign milestone, not completed work.
Establish or improve outposts using transported workers, materials, machinery,
fuel, construction wages and operating capital. Fund escorts and supply routes.
Blockades, defenses, capture, withdrawal, treaties and peace can change control.
An ownership change preserves physical assets, damage, workers, stores and
accounts; it does not duplicate fleets, mint coins or reset a settlement.
Record territorial changes and their reasons so the player can understand them.

BRASS TIDE - OPEN OCEAN VERSION MAP v0.063-v0.072
28 October 2026 | v0.063 VERIFIED; v0.064-v0.072 PLANNED

PURPOSE
Build a large ocean world from smaller, detailed island groups. Preserve the
Shatter Coast as the starting inhabited region. Sailing offshore leads to a
playable ocean chart; approaching another group opens its local waters. Combat
continues on a separate encounter map reflecting the actual location.

WORLD DESIGN
- Settled core: busy faction harbors, production, fishing and trade routes.
- Frontier: scattered settlements, disputed waters and pirate anchorages.
- Distant archipelagos: discoveries, ruins, wrecks and resources worth a voyage.
- Sector design target: 48 groups, comprising 8 settled, 12 frontier and 28 remote
  groups. This is a proposed content target, not existing or accepted content.
  Groups vary in size and character; remote groups generally have no full port.
  Establish useful destinations and route choices before expanding their count.
- A visible fleet travels on the ocean chart. Routes can be changed or interrupted
  by pursuit, sightings and discoveries. Local and offshore travel share one clock,
  physical fleet, inventory, crew, damage state and conserved accounts.
- Transition at coastal approaches, with a choice of arrival side. No magical
  jump gates, automatic hostile-fleet escape, free refueling or reset purses.

0.064 - TWO-SCALE WORLD AND TRANSITIONS
Scope: world-region identity, an ocean chart, the existing Shatter Coast and one
remote prototype group. Sail out, choose an approach, enter and return. Save/load
correctly from either layer. Keep existing voyages and their local coordinates.
Acceptance: a round trip retains original hull/equipment IDs, cargo, coins, crew,
damage and time. No double ticking, duplicated fleets, reset encounters or free
escape by changing layers. A prototype destination is not a finished expedition.

0.065 - LONG-VOYAGE PREPARATION AND COSTS
Scope: distance/time estimates, fuel, rations, wages, maintenance, cargo capacity
and explicit return reserves. Existing tankers and support boats extend endurance
through actual carried stores and transfers. Fuel also remains necessary locally.
Acceptance: forecast and actual voyage accounting agree under stated conditions;
speed changes affect travel time and costs appropriately. Low reserves are clear
before departure; running short uses paid assistance or recovery, not free stores.

0.066 - PLAYABLE OPEN-OCEAN TRAVEL
Scope: route changes, currents, weather zones, sightings, pursuit and interception.
Use inexpensive chart effects and route modifiers, not simulated ocean fluids.
Persistent fleet contacts can lead to battles in actual offshore encounter space.
Acceptance: player can avoid a threat, investigate a contact, divert and resume;
both sides obey movement, detection, stores and continuous pursuit/transition rules.
No rebuilding tactical AI or adding unrelated weather-damage systems in this pass.

0.067 - FIRST REMOTE EXPLORATION ARCHIPELAGO
Scope: finish the prototype group as a worthwhile expedition: distinct coastline,
surveyable sites, a clue chain, a valuable discovery, salvage and a return decision.
Reuse the existing surveying, contacts and recovery systems across regional IDs.
Acceptance: discover, investigate, choose what fits aboard and bring it home.
Unknown information stays unknown. Recovery uses real capacity, crew, time and
supplies; a revealed resource is not an automatically granted cargo reward.

0.068 - INTERREGIONAL SHIPPING AND DISTANT SIMULATION
Scope: extend existing NPC objectives and accounting across ocean/local layers.
Merchant fleets deliver real cargo; pirates intercept; patrols respond; survivors
return for paid service. Offscreen regions advance on scheduled data updates.
Acceptance: one shipment can depart, be intercepted or arrive and alter inventories
exactly once. Joining a region preserves its physical fleets, losses and accounts.
Bounded worker jobs publish safe data; never run full battles everywhere at once.

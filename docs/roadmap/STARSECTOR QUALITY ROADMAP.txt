THE TARGET
A naval fleet RPG with the presentation, responsiveness, tactical decisions,
fleet-building satisfaction and campaign engagement the owner expects from
Starsector. That is the minimum design target, not an achievement claim.
Adding similarly named features does not meet it. Existing systems are
prototypes until their behavior and presentation work together in actual play.
Version numbers record delivered work; they do not certify quality.

The intended loop:
Find an opportunity -> prepare an affordable fleet -> choose a route and risk
-> fight, trade, explore or negotiate -> recover and bring something worthwhile
home -> make a meaningful fitting/fleet decision -> discover a new opportunity.
Each link must be pleasant and understandable. A large economic simulation
behind a dull battle or an unreadable map does not complete the loop.

WHERE WE ACTUALLY ARE
Delivered v0.041 has 36 hull definitions and foundations for refitting, damage,
crew, labor/coins, trading, exploration, recovery, contacts, factions and NPC
operations. These are starting material, not proof of Starsector-level play.
Current boats, guns, coast and settlements still look like prototype assets.
More polygon subdivisions, color changes and repeated house stamps are not
an adequate art direction. The current campaign needs a designed visual world.

The measured 42-boat/294DP-per-side opening uses 21.703ms median per update,
P95 41.126ms, in a 0.2667s headless sample under the recorded test limits.
That is not sustained combat or displayed FPS. The separate 1.6s four-boat
sample and eight-second convoy fixture are also too short to certify game feel.
The existing 380 checks establish selected behavior, not artistic quality,
replayability, command satisfaction or a finished campaign.

LOCKED DESIGN
- Godot, Windows PC first. Android compatibility is secondary.
- A strategic coastal chart and separate isometric tactical battles.
- Default flagship autopilot; WASD camera, pause and clear tactical orders.
- 300 deployment points per side, separate from total ownership and storage.
- Original boats, weapons, factions, writing, art and geography. Do not copy
  Blood Wake or Starsector assets, names, maps or exact designs.
- Light water rendering. Use inexpensive prepared textures, restrained surface
  motion and bounded effects. No expensive water simulation requirement.
- Numerical crews. Visible helm/gunners are part of the boat asset.
- Coins, real material and labor costs, finite stores and conserved purses.
  Keep the owner's fictional wage anchor of 100 coins per sailor-day.
- Preserve saves and one unambiguous latest-build desktop launcher.

QUALITY MILESTONES
A: 0.042-0.051 - Prove a good-looking, responsive and replayable core game.
B: 0.052-0.056 - Make fleet progression and the living coast worth exploring.
C: 0.057-0.061 - Make the careers, consequences and long campaign hold together.
Do not advance into more campaign breadth to conceal an unfinished milestone A.
A failed milestone creates a bounded correction to its actual failing part;
it does not trigger an open-ended full-game rewrite or another test framework.
These are work scopes, not a guarantee that parity arrives at a particular
version number or date. Split a genuinely oversized scope transparently into
further .001 releases; never mark unfinished work complete to match the table.

0.042 - REPLACE PLACEHOLDER BOAT ART
Produce three finished representative boats: starter skiff, armed escort and
large merchant. Set one consistent scale, lighting, material palette, silhouette
and viewing angle. Build a dependable asset pipeline: authored detailed models
or painted directional sprites, baked offline into inexpensive runtime assets.
Keep hulls separate from interchangeable guns, mounts and damage overlays.
Prepare authored engine/outboard units, fittings, rails and deck machinery;
avoid thin generic rods, black-disc guns and recolored copies of one hull.
PROOF: the actual game shows all three at normal battle and refit zoom, with
correct mount positions, readable identity and no clipping or floating parts.
A concept sheet or an impressive close-up alone does not finish this version.

0.043 - REAL RENDERED PERFORMANCE AND RUNTIME BUDGET
Measure the actual exported game in active mixed and light-boat 300-vs-300
battles, not only an opening without shots. Include movement, gunfire, missiles,
flooding and effects. Record hardware, resolution, renderer and actor counts.
Use event-driven equipment updates, shared frame data, spatial broad phases,
bounded/staggered tactical decisions and immutable worker jobs where profiling
shows a benefit. Keep authoritative physics/contacts live; workers do not touch
scene/UI objects. Retain existing threaded rendering and economy workers.
Do not halve actor counts, physics frequency or useful AI to win a chart.
PROOF TARGET on the owner's PC: 60 FPS at the agreed native play resolution,
95 percent of active frames <=20ms, and no recurring simulation catch-up or
50ms+ stutters. Planning simulation budget: median <=6ms, P95 <=10ms, leaving
room for rendering and presentation. These are proposed targets, not measured
current results. A separate normal runtime profile is needed: capped headless
regressions cannot establish this. Record memory/VRAM and loading stalls too.

0.044 - COMBAT WITH PRESSURE, OPENINGS AND RECOVERY
Tune acceleration, turning, stopping distance, weapon arcs, projectile travel,
range and time to kill together. Boats need weight and responsive commands.
Use reload windows, limited salvos, positioning, exposed engines and flooding/
damage-control workload to create commitment, danger and opportunities to
break away. A light craft must have a useful advantage over a larger hull.
Choose a coherent pressure/recovery loop before adding more meters. Do not
blindly copy space shields/flux or pile on heat and fatigue systems.
PROOF: duels and small mixed fights contain deliberate firing passes, punishable
mistakes and possible recoveries, rather than two stationary health bars racing.

0.045 - WEAPONS, IMPACTS, WAKES AND AUDIO
Give machine guns, cannons, rockets and torpedoes distinct authored models,
mount sizes, barrels, feeds/breeches, traverse, recoil and firing behavior.
Synchronize muzzle flash, tracer/projectile, impact and sound with the actual
weapon event. Make armor hits, penetrations, splashes, fires and sinking clear.
Build a restrained stern wash that follows actual travel and fades naturally;
remove ghost hull shadows, long geometric wake rails and obvious bubble chains.
Use licensed recorded weapon/engine sounds, bounded layering, distance and
volume mixing. Integrate the supplied music without drowning out information.
PROOF: each family is distinguishable by sight and sound in normal combat;
effects do not obscure aiming or cause measurable frame spikes. Silent tests
cannot sign off the sound mix, and cosmetic explosions cannot invent kills.

0.046 - TACTICAL AI THAT DESERVES AUTOPILOT
Light boats flank, orbit, harass, bait a turn, exploit a distracted heavy and
break away before being trapped. Strike boats time torpedo/rocket passes.
Heavies hold useful arcs and anchor space; escorts intercept threats to a
carrier or wounded ally rather than all chasing the same target. Support
boats avoid the front. Coordinate pressure and targets without perfect knowledge.
Consider fitted weapon range, ammunition, mobility, damage and escape room.
PROOF: the same six short scenario classes show useful behavior on both sides:
flanker vs heavy; escort vs raider; strike pass; pressured withdrawal; wounded
ally cover; mixed fleet. No persistent indecision, spinning or friendly traffic
jams. An order may be declined for physical danger, with a visible reason.

0.047 - FLEET COMMAND AND BATTLE READABILITY
A clean deployment screen, reserves, selected-boat information, weapon arcs,
range/threat information on demand, clear orders and objective/escape markers.
Support engage, cautious pressure/harass, focus attack, escort, defend, rally,
hold and withdraw with distinct intent. Give formation/group commands useful
meaning. Keep the battle visible; large permanent panels must not eat it.
PROOF: selection and pause are reliable; commands acknowledge immediately and
new intent normally begins within 0.25s, apart from physical turning/braking.
The player can tell who is shooting whom and why a boat is disengaging.

0.048 - REFITTING THAT CHANGES HOW A BOAT PLAYS
A proper visual workshop: select physical mounts, inspect compatibility/arcs,
compare performance and costs, assign weapon groups and save/apply loadouts.
Engine, armor, pumps, ammunition and fittings create meaningful tradeoffs in
speed, protection, endurance, firing arcs and damage-control capacity.
Keep construction/refit labor, material costs and scarce stock real. Explain
what a change does before the captain pays for it. Use the existing lab to try it.
PROOF: at least three deliberately different builds of one suitable hull behave
differently in the same encounter. Stronger equipment has a meaningful cost;
there is no universally best shopping-list build.

0.049 - THE WHOLE ROSTER REACHES THE ART AND ROLE STANDARD
Bring the existing 24 combat boats and 12 civilian designs through the proven
asset pipeline. Review silhouettes, materials, motors, mount sizes, previews,
damage states and representative headings at normal zoom. Distinguish classes
and factions without relying on color alone. Finish civilian holds/tanks/fishing
gear and yacht identity. Give every hull a useful role or revise it.
PROOF: no placeholder hull or gun remains in the shipped roster; small boats
remain useful alongside large warships; existing boats are not just portraits
sharing a generic live combat asset. Do not add filler hulls to claim breadth.

0.050 - A COAST WORTH LOOKING AT AND NAVIGATING
Replace bare land polygons and identical building grids with a designed coastal
world: recognizable headlands, beaches/cliffs, reefs, vegetation, sea routes and
harbors with distinct street/roof layouts and industry landmarks. Show shipyards,
fishing towns, foundries and wealthy passenger ports as different places.
Use inexpensive authored map/terrain art, sensible zoom levels and readable
fleet/port presentation. Actual navigable land and its art must agree.
PROOF: at default zoom, place, route and destination are readable and attractive.
Regional art, harbor panels and battle terrain feel like the same world. Known
exploration opportunities and traffic make the chart useful, not empty decoration.

0.051 - FIRST-VOYAGE QUALITY MILESTONE
Connect title, new captain, harbor, fitting, route, encounter, result, salvage,
repair and return into one polished opening. Start with the cheap boat and a
clear opportunity, enough reserves and choices that do not require a manual.
A successful first voyage must pay for operation and enable a meaningful next
fitting/fleet decision, not bury progress under wages and mandatory repairs.
PROOF: the first 30 minutes make the next voyage appealing, the player can explain
why a fight was won/lost, and buying a second boat is an understandable goal.
A second run supports a different useful fitting or route. Art, command feel,
AI and actual rendered performance must all clear milestone A. Assertion counts
and eight seconds of autopilot do not substitute for this experience.

0.052 - SATISFYING FLEET GROWTH AND LOGISTICS
Turn existing cargo, fuel, crew, wages, repair stocks, storage and civilian hulls
into comprehensible expedition preparation. Show operating days, return reserve,
payload and the effect of adding a support boat. Keep ownership separate from
battle deployment. Explain losses, morale, arrears, recovery and rescue loans.
PROOF: cheap boat -> specialist partner -> capable mixed flotilla is viable;
a tanker, transport or repair/support boat extends an expedition in a visible
way. A 300-point deployment is a tactical choice, not a total ownership ceiling.

0.053 - TRADE THAT CONNECTS THE LTV ECONOMY TO PLAYER DECISIONS
Use the existing real purses, households, labor, production recipes, priorities
and inventories. Present useful market information: dated prices, demand,
shortages, delivery needs, voyage costs and expected risk-adjusted margin.
Make deliveries change actual production and local buying opportunities.
PROOF: the captain can find a profitable cargo, pay wages/fuel, deliver it, and
observe a supplied shipyard or recovering shortage. Firms, households and the
government still pay from their actual purses. More accounting screens alone
are not a trade game, and arbitrary boat prices are not acceptable.

0.054 - INDEPENDENT CAMPAIGN FLEETS WITH BELIEVABLE PURPOSE
Finish observable merchants, escorts, patrols, pirates and hostile faction
fleets pursuing actual cargo, protection, raids and operations. Use sightings,
route costs, risk and supply; avoid omniscient pursuit of the player.
Fleets fight each other without the player nearby. Off-screen resolution must
preserve roster, damage, cargo, people, ammunition and repair/replacement costs,
while remaining much cheaper than simulating every distant battle at 60Hz.
PROOF: follow one real shipment, raid and patrol response from departure to
survivors, delivered/stolen cargo and funded repair/replacement. It must still
happen when the player sails elsewhere. Explain sightings/events in useful intel.

0.055 - FACTIONS, CONTACTS AND OPPORTUNITY
Make faction identity visible in art, equipment, doctrine, trade interests and
behavior. Explain hostility, inspections, commissions, restricted access,
smuggling consequences and rewards. Contacts remember meaningful witnessed
work and offer compatible jobs; intelligence has an age and location.
PROOF: choosing whom to help changes concrete prices/access/jobs/safety or
faction support. Rival factions fight for understandable reasons. Reputation
is more than an unexplained number or a random attack switch.

0.056 - EXPLORATION WITH DISCOVERY AND PAYOFF
Complete the postponed original island expedition, then establish reusable
content patterns without a sprawling new story engine: clues, survey stages,
resource finds, hidden anchorages, abandoned facilities and disputed salvage.
Mix authored surprises with replay variation. Tie discovery to useful equipment,
trade leads, recovery, contacts or access, not only generic coin awards.
PROOF: one voyage combines a job, a worthwhile discovery and an optional risk;
a clue changes the next destination. The player can choose how far to push
before supplies and the return journey become dangerous. Clear milestone B.

0.057 - SALVAGE, PRIZES AND RECOVERY AS REAL CHOICES
Make post-battle recovery fast to understand and consequential: recover a hull,
remove valuable machinery/weapons, strip supplies, tow or leave a prize.
Cargo space, towing performance, repair crews, danger and equipment condition
shape the choice. Preserve actual custody and module identities.
PROOF: a poor captain can make a memorable fleet upgrade from a difficult
recovery, while stripping a wreck can be the smarter choice on another trip.
No reward screen silently duplicates a boat, crew, cargo or item.

0.058 - CIVILIAN CAREERS THAT REMAIN INTERESTING
Polish fishing, freight, fuel delivery and passenger/yacht work using the real
civilian roster and existing funded jobs. Add route/risk choices, useful
specialization, recurring clients and demand, not four reskinned delivery buttons.
Cargo and passenger value create actual piracy/escort opportunities.
PROOF: each civilian career pays its operating costs, progresses to a useful
larger/different vessel and provides decisions beyond waiting for a timer.
A merchant or fisher is a supported way to play, not a combat career's chore.

0.059 - CAPTAINS, CREWS AND FLEET ATTACHMENT
Use paid officers/specialists, crew skill, morale, wounds and boat histories
to make a flotilla personal. Doctrine and fitting should agree with actual AI
behavior. Keep people numerical; do not introduce crew animation work.
Present training, recruitment, wages and losses clearly through existing labor
and purse systems. Avoid mandatory micro-management of every sailor.
PROOF: two officers/builds of a comparable boat can be tactically distinct;
an experienced vessel has a useful history and losing it matters without
making recovery or a new captain's progression unreasonable.

0.060 - A REGION THAT CHANGES AND A REASON TO STAY
Connect supplied faction operations, blockades, relief, harbor development,
optional storage/business/home harbor and regional story consequences.
Use actual goods, workers, capital and shipping to enable expansion or repair.
Keep long-term objectives aligned with the fleet RPG; do not turn this into an
unrelated nation simulator. Make larger civilian targets and sustained conflict
create valuable opportunities for explorers, merchants and privateers alike.
PROOF: the player can help secure a corridor or develop a harbor and see the
result in traffic, safety, inventories and faction opportunity. Operations
stall or fail for explained resource/strategic reasons, not arbitrary resets.

0.061 - WHOLE-GAME QUALITY MILESTONE
Bring the art, sound, interfaces, progression, economy, AI, exploration, recovery
and regional consequences together. Review early/mid/late fleet compositions,
all six careers and differing faction relationships. Finish practical onboarding,
settings, save/load/migration, clear failure recovery and content variety.
Assess actual gameplay recordings and play feedback alongside focused tests.
PROOF: there are compelling reasons for the next battle, next refit, next boat
and next expedition; the careers remain viable; the campaign creates memorable
choices and consequences; current full-battle rendered performance meets target.
If any pillar still fails the intended quality level, keep that milestone open
and plan the specific remaining work. Do not declare 'as good as Starsector'
because this version number or the end of a checklist has been reached.

HOW WORK IS EXECUTED
Each release has one written scope and observable play result. Do the art and
behavior design before broad implementation. Helpers own separate files/tasks
under the user's chosen Bridge team when implementation is authorized. Root
reviews integration and actual game presentation, rather than farming out
unrelated micro-features and calling their isolated checks a complete game.
The first three boats establish the standard before producing the other 33.
Use the existing project maps, developer tools, laboratory, queue and short
benchmark tooling. No new coordination, certification or test-framework project.
Existing features should be finished/reworked where needed rather than built
again under new names. Keep the next-version scope small enough to complete;
larger milestones span the listed increments.

VALIDATION AND SCHEDULING
The entire selected automated batch stays below120seconds, including setup,
import, export, startup, measurements and cleanup. Choose a few useful cases;
do not evade this rule with endless repeat batches or the old full-game matrix.
Use the machine-wide read-only detector and shared FIFO. Queue behind other
Godot/Unity editors, players, exported games and leases. Never close or control
someone else's application. No mouse, keyboard or foreground test takeover.
Any GPU/rendered capture must use authorized background/offscreen facilities
and the same shared lane; planning does not authorize a GPU run or a download.
Preserve the existing test resource limits unless explicitly changed through
the supported authorization path. The short capped regression profile and a
normal-runtime rendered performance profile are distinct and must be labeled.
CPU primitive captures, logic checks and source advisories cannot sign off
rendered FPS, sound listening or fun. Use actual rendered assets/gameplay and
recorded weapon sounds for those reviews when execution is authorized. Do not
require a new user approval for every ordinary source edit or small milestone.
Longer experiential review is gameplay feedback, not an excuse for a background
multi-hour automated soak or a test suite that exceeds the two-minute rule.

DESIGN REFERENCE
The owner's Starsector experience is the primary experiential benchmark.
Fractal Softworks' historical official manual documents differentiated combat
assignments such as harass, strike, escort and civilian rally. Those illustrate
why fleet roles and understandable commands matter; this plan applies the
principle to motorboats with the owner's control preferences, rather than
claiming the old manual describes all current Starsector behavior or balance.
https://www.fractalsoftworks.com/starfarer/docs/StarfarerManual.pdf
The roadmap's quality targets and naval adaptations are our proposed design,
not performance measurements or promises made by Fractal Softworks.

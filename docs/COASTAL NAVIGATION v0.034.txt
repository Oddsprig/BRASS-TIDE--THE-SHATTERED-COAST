The coast now shapes every voyage and fight. Your routes are charted for the largest hulls you actually have under way, so a big, wide-turning boat is sent through water it can truly clear while a nimble skiff can still work the tighter passages between the named reefs and shoals. When you sail into a harbor, your approach stays in safe water within reach of harbor service the whole way in — no final dash across the shallows and no course that snaps back toward the shore. Battles take place on the coast where they actually happen: fight near a named shoal and its reefs crowd the water, while an encounter in genuinely open water is fought on empty sea. Fleets form up and convoy boats make for their exits only where the water is safe, so boats never appear aground on land that was never part of that stretch of coast. If that stretch of coast cannot safely hold the fleets, connect the two sides, or give the loaded boats a reachable exit, the engagement is refused with the reason shown instead of starting in unsafe water.

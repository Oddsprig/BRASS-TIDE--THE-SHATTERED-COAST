BRASS TIDE v0.059 - CHOOSING CIVILIAN WORK

Open Civilian voyages from a harbor. Review a particular offered load before
accepting it. The review compares the contracted fee with outward travel,
handling, return travel, all owned crews' wages and replacement food/fuel.
Contingency reserves are money to keep available, separate from expected costs.
Open cost assumptions to inspect the individual tanks and calendar payroll.
Costs exclude unknown combat damage, delays and losses; an estimate pays nothing.

Freight moves existing sealed cargo against an actual destination stock gap.
Small and larger loads can serve different real holds. Freight still uses the
shared traveling manifest; inspecting one hull does not assign freight to it.
Fuel and passengers do require the selected physical carrier. Mounted gear,
lockers, loaded stores, spare berths and return supplies all take real room.

A fuel recipient can move or obtain fuel before you arrive. The report explains
its current presence and need. Review the route and uncertainty before reserving
your cargo space. No fee is paid for an unavailable recipient. Active work keeps
its accepted terms; newly quoted operating-tank jobs do not alter old contracts.
Ordinary arriving fleets make a finite quarter-day harbor service stop, then
resume their own work. The stop is not reserved for your delivery. Presence
requires an active, stopped fleet at a safe actual harbor approach.

Passenger charters move existing civilians, their bought food and their savings
to real destination housing. Their savings are custody and are never your income.
Paid client memories reflect retained completed transactions, not a free bonus.

Fishing begins at suitable water after stopping, with actual assigned sailors.
Choose a work horizon and a buyer from your recorded harbors. Review the finite
ground's daily quota, catch rate, hold, working fuel and food, and return route.
The catch forecast is a maximum; other boats can fish the ground and buyer funds
or prices may change. Sell the actual returned whole lots through the Market.

Buying a civilian boat costs the hull's real ready price, trained crew advances
and supplies. Keep those capital costs separate from one voyage's operating
surplus. A useful trip can contribute toward another vessel; it does not prove
an entire career's progression. Fleet ownership and support boats remain separate
from the 300-point battle deployment limit. Autopilot and WASD camera remain.

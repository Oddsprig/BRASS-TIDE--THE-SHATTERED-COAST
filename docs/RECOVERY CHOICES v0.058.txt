BRASS TIDE v0.058 - WHAT TO BRING HOME

An unclaimed wreck is still a place in the world. Approach it, stop the fleet,
recall any shore party and secure the waters before working. Leaving it marked
takes no goods and does no recovery work. A captured boat is different: it is
already in your custody, even if its hold is empty. Closing this board does not
dispose of an owned boat.

Collecting loose stores takes only the finite amount actually aboard, bounded
by your traveling hold. Taking structural material sacrifices the hull. A tow
pays actual tug labor and inspection, preserves damaged fittings and brings
back an uncrewed support hull. It slows your flotilla; its theoretical cargo
capacity is not available until it is repaired and ready to travel.

Compare the work and the route home before agreeing. Reserve crew wages, food
and each working boat's fuel. The quoted later harbor service is subject to
actual kits, materials, mechanics and shipwright availability; today's stock
is not a reservation for your arrival. A hull-repair estimate alone does not
price every damaged gun or engine. Crew, working stores and new fitting work
remain separate purchases.

Selected gun or machinery recovery keeps that exact physical item's identity,
damage and original design. Choose an existing working boat's locker. An item
in a locker consumes hold space on that particular boat; another boat's empty
hold does not move the locker. Space remains occupied on later trade and after
reload. Weight uses the existing material recipes in game hold units, not a
claim about historically exact weapon mass. Installed fittings are not counted
again as loose cargo.

Removing selected machinery permanently gives up the wreck's hull, all other
fittings and all remaining structural material lots. Review that complete loss
before accepting. The remaining loose stores can still be collected separately.
This choice prevents receiving the same machinery both as a real gun and again
as structural materials. Integral pumps cannot be extracted into a locker.

Recovered does not mean repaired or installed. The ordinary workshop checks
mount size, design compatibility, fitting points, materials, labor and payment.
Original stock equipment may be kept but cannot be installed on a different
hull design merely because it was recovered. Sometimes a small supply lot is
the useful choice; sometimes preparing enough money, crew and return reserves
lets you bring home a lasting fleet upgrade.

BRASS TIDE v0.070 - WORK, VOYAGES AND FLEET GROWTH

Stop at a known harbor's actual berth to review funded work. A client pays
from that harbor's existing purse. Accepting freight purchases real exportable
stock and seals it in your hold; delivering it adds those goods at the destination.
Your fee stays in escrow until you complete the agreed work. Client funds are
not preparation money. Several compatible jobs can share one voyage.

Select a job to review its route and current-fleet estimate. Gross fee is the
payment before wages, provisions, fuel and repair stores. Expected costs cover
the whole remaining outward journey, work and return. The return reserve is
an overlapping allowance with a contingency; do not subtract it a second time.
Delays, damage, extra boats and extra crew can change the outcome.

Every sailor aboard earns wages, including spare hands. A full survey needs
four spare sailors beyond the crew required to operate the boats. The starting
Tinwake needs three to sail, so seven aboard can operate it and complete a
full survey. Survey parties still consume actual kits, rations and paid time.

Load loose fuel, ammunition and kits from the hold into boat tanks, magazines
and repair racks before leaving. Hold room and passenger accommodation are
different limits. Sealed freight still takes space if its contract expires;
unload or return it through the existing abandonment action.

Existing boats and paid conversions have useful tradeoffs. Cargo Rigs enlarge
the hold at the expense of speed and handling. Survey Rigs speed shore work
while reducing hold space. Workshops improve crew repairs and still consume
kits. Tug Rigs improve towing and pumping. Auxiliary Tanks improve endurance
and fuel use while taking hold space. Read the actual quote for materials,
worker wages, expert time, price and the fitted boat's resulting statistics.
Paid specialists offer another step in fleet growth. A surveyor improves shore
work and occupies a real crew berth. Hiring moves an existing harbor worker
aboard and pays an advance; their base wage and professional premium continue.

Transports carry larger loads; tankers support fuel work; yachts offer passenger
accommodation. Work requires the actual suitable boat, supplies and real client
or recipient. Remote passenger charters stay within one local island group.
Existing escort and recovery activities require real loaded boats or wrecks;
opening the board does not create a convoy, wreck, cargo or reward.

Crew recruitment, shipyards, refitting and fleet storage remain home-coast
services. Regional work desks do not grant those services remotely. Bring the
same boats, crew and earnings home, replace consumed stores, then choose a
useful improvement whose full labor and material costs you can afford.

Existing careers and the single story contract remain available. A fresh
campaign uses the 48-group world; old two-region saves retain their world.

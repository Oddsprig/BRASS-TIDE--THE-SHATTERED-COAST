BRASS TIDE v0.030 - CURRENT SOURCE CONTROLS; UNTESTED
Tests deferred AT OWNER REQUEST. Finish Windows BUILD ONLY, then STOP.
These controls describe the saved source; no new gameplay/UI acceptance is claimed.

Combat starts on autopilot. WASD pans the camera; Shift speeds panning. Scroll
zooms and Home centers. Click/drag selects boats; Shift adds to the selection.
Right-click issues move, attack or escort. 1/2/3 issue engage/escort/hold.
Space pauses; Tab opens tactical overview and restores prior pause/control mode
on exit. U toggles manual flagship control; arrows and mouse steer/fire manually.
U/manual can retake the flagship even after fleet withdrawal is queued.

Open Selected tactics & weapon groups for doctrine, role, formation, protected
ally, rally, local withdrawal and per-mount automatic hold/reserve choices.
Manual fire bypasses voluntary automatic hold/reserve but still needs actual
crew, ammunition, cooldown and physical aim/clearance. Ctrl+F1-F4 saves a selected
group and F1-F4 recalls it during that battle. No new battle-save promise.

Withdraw fleet uses the legacy 12-second compatibility escape timer. Local
rally/survival movement is physical and does not settle fleet retreat. Further
physical objective exits and .031-.038 work are stopped. Previous guide follows.

HISTORICAL RECORDS - superseded for current authorization/status

v0.020 — HARBOR RESERVES AND USEFUL TRADE
Enter a harbor and choose Supply coverage & protected stocks. Coverage is days of actual household/recipe/home-fleet use. Commercial stock, public stores and inbound physical cargo remain distinct. A modest operating buffer is kept for essentials, while actual surplus is available to both captains and NPC companies. Incoming cargo is not already in stock.
A Friendly harbor partner can advise commercial or supply-security policy. A paid granary request transfers up to one day of actual public food into the commercial store;25clerk+22oversight coins go to the people doing the work. It does not put free food into your fleet. Essential production gets bounded feasible priority within existing paid labor and available materials.
Course wage validation also checks paid progress and monotonic identities; fractional elapsed time settles every held coin at completion.

v0.019 — PAID CREW TRAINING
In harbor choose Crew training & harbor workforce. Qualified recruits can join a real crew berth immediately; hiring consumes both a resident worker and that eligibility. A course trains existing unqualified workers and reserves one available instructor as well. The full quote shows provisions, repair/survey kits, trainee pay100/day, professional instruction, oversight, facility costs and duty. Material lots and setup costs are spent when commissioned; wage coins remain held and are earned over actual economic days.
Courses take4days for sailors,6for gunners and8for mechanics. Completion returns people to the civilian workforce with qualification eligibility; specialist courses also increase the town's trained expertise. Cancellation returns people and only unspent wages. Existing crew competence is preserved. Selling/storing boats and discharging professionals return real people and their savings ashore. Current courses persist through save/load.

BRASS TIDE — THE SHATTER COAST
Captain's campaign guide | v0.018

START SMALL
Continue preserves your existing voyage. New career begins with a Tinwake
skiff, five paid sailors and 15,000 coins. The eight original contracts remain
available; exploring, trading and building a life ashore are optional careers.
The fleet laboratory on the title screen is isolated from career money/saves.

WASD pans the camera. Shift pans faster. Scroll zooms. Home returns to your
fleet; M shows the territory. F opens Fleet, C Hold, J Contracts, I Island
charts, K Voyage jobs and L Regional goals. Escape opens the captain's menu.
Your flagship begins on autopilot. Pause with Space; issue orders while paused.

PREPARE TO RETURN
Open Prepare expedition before a long passage. Read hold space, actual crew
pay, food, fuel and round-trip reserves. An ordinary sailor earns 100 coins per
day; food is separate. Missed wages become arrears and affect morale. Sailors'
savings are their purse, not spending money for the captain.

Working tanks, magazines and kit racks have explicit capacities. Extra stores,
trade goods and sealed freight consume dry hold space. A sunk carrier loses its
allocated goods. You can jettison owned cargo onto a finite marked float.
Sealed freight belongs to its sponsor and cannot be donated to your business.

You may own more than 300 deployment points. Each normal battle still fields
up to 300 points per side. Stored hulls remain at their actual harbor.
Traveling support reserves contribute their hold and fitted support benefits
without deploying as warships. Paid storage returns sailors and savings ashore;
discharge paid specialists before putting their boat into storage.

FOLLOW A LEAD
Approach a coastal landmark to sight it, then open I. Charting and full surveys
need spare able sailors, time, food and tools. Reserve enough sailors above the
boats' minimum crews. Stop close to the site while a shore party works; recall
it before departing. Following a lead can reveal an anchorage, resources,
machinery, a wreck field or another useful location.

Surveys reveal knowledge and opportunities. They do not conjure stock into your
hold. Four coastal story chains connect those discoveries to harbor contacts,
actual deliveries, local hostility and a choice with persistent consequences.
Read the story journal's next location. Some endings open a unique funded
freight passage, public/private salvage rights or a workshop charter.

CHOOSE WHAT TO SAVE
Destroyed boats leave individual persistent wrecks. Approach and stop to inspect
them. Recover useful equipment, strip selected material lots or pay for a tow.
Stripping structure rules out recovering the same whole hull. Space, spare
hands and time decide what can come home. A towed hull is damaged, uncrewed and
slows the flotilla; it needs paid harbor repairs before active service.

MAKE ONE VOYAGE DO SEVERAL JOBS
K holds up to six compatible funded jobs alongside your main contract. Freight
occupies real space aboard actual carriers. Sponsors buy/allocate physical
goods and move payment into escrow. Survey jobs and dated leads can align with
the same route. Deadlines matter; damaged or lost freight cannot be paid twice.
Meet harbor contacts and keep track of personal trust separately from faction
reputation. Market reports are dated observations, not live omniscient prices.

THE COAST MOVES
Convoys buy and sell actual inventory. Patrols escort/intercept; raiders seek
prey. They spend wages, rations, fuel and ammunition, can retreat and repair,
and need real money, workers and materials to replace losses. A supplied
hostile fleet can blockade a port; withdrawal, starvation, defeat or defenders
can lift it. Delivering missing iron can make a real shipyard commission viable.

Peaceful traders are not free targets. Attacking them creates explained
reputation losses, witnessed offenses and harbor claims. Legal neutral ports
offer basic civilian services. Advanced military equipment needs Friendly
standing. Safe conduct is a paid, limited civil agreement. A commission is an
explicit commitment with stated enemies; it never creates free stipend coins.

GIVE BOATS A JOB
All 24 hull designs remain available through appropriate yards and discovery.
Weapons fit physical interchangeable sockets; engines and armor are paid fits.
Interior conversions trade hold and speed for cargo, surveying, repair support,
towing or reserve fuel. Paid specialists occupy real crew berths and receive
professional wages in addition to their ordinary crew share. Unpaid premiums
become arrears and suspend benefits. Service histories remember actual voyages.

BUILD A HOME
At a legal harbor, open Home harbors & warehouses. A warehouse hires two real
workers, stores owned goods ashore and includes eight small-boat berth slots
while its staff are paid. Larger boats occupy more slots. Public storage is
28 coins per slot/day, paid to the harbor watch/registry. Unpaid rent becomes a
visible liability; your hull is preserved, but pay locally before collection.

Specialist businesses require full surveys and appropriate story-earned rights.
They buy setup materials, reserve construction/supervision workers, hire staff
out of the town and hold their own working capital. Move goods and company
coins at that actual home port. Workshops consume timber, iron and carpenter
work to make repair kits. Retain output for the fleet or sell to a solvent
local buyer. Wage shortfalls pause work; no workers, inputs or coins appear
for free.

Goods you deposit have standard production-cost basis. Capital and deposited
goods are not earned profits. Dividends require actual sales surplus after pay
and sold-material cost, with fourteen days of staff wages reserved.

SET A LONGER GOAL — L
Corridor: choose two harbors and organize the goal at either endpoint.
Keep the approaches and lane clear for seven consecutive economic days, with
a real merchant passage, local escort/victory or two actual endpoint visits
by the assigned patrol. A Friendly local government may fund up to seven
wage-days for an existing patrol while retaining two public-payroll days.
Its boats still sail, recover and resupply normally; they never spawn beside
you. A renewed threat resets consecutive clear days.

Harbor: choose and visit the harbor, then begin its development goal. Contribute
or sell twelve owned material lots after starting. A shore business there must
pay three full daily payrolls. Existing materials, staff and purses determine
what development is affordable.

Explorer: fully survey three connected sites by following their leads.
Previously completed full surveys count as earned progress; charting alone
does not qualify.

Peacemaker: choose hostile factions. Deliver five food or repair-kit lots to
EACH side, earn Friendly standing with one party and legal access to both, and
clear witnessed offenses and claims. Allow three economic days for talks after
both relief deliveries. Sign at either party's legal harbor. The neutral truce
lasts thirty days; the previous dispute resumes unless later diplomacy has
superseded it. New talks must wait through the stated cooldown. Your personal
reputation and commission remain separate from the faction-pair agreement.

Goals are optional and can run together. Withdraw one to change direction;
delivered goods and grants remain with their recipients, and a restart cannot
duplicate treasury support. Completed routes/harbors retain stable records.

PORTABILITY AND TESTING
Keep the executable with its PCK, music, saves and docs folders. Older version
folders remain independent. In-game Development notes and the adjacent
DEVELOPER NOTES.txt describe the current build. Automated checks run hidden or
headless, with no desktop input. Numerical and CPU results do not replace your
judgment about control feel, presentation, listening or campaign fun.

RECOVER MACHINERY AND DEVELOP A WORKSHOP — v0.021
Fully survey abandoned workshops or salvage anchorages, then open Recovered
machinery from Hold or a harbor. Land two spare sailors, commit real kits and
remain within reach for the stated paid days. Their ordinary crew payroll pays
them once. Claim the finite equipment, keep its hold space free and bring it
home. Losing its carrier loses that machine; the source stays exhausted.

At an owned workshop, install compatible tooling with actual materials, wages
and reserved staff. Commissioning takes elapsed days. Paid maintenance improves
one named labor bottleneck by at most20%; inputs, staff and storage still limit
output. Machinery is optional. Restore or remove the same item; consumed setup
costs never return as a refund. Change workshop recipes between repair kits,
planks, engine assemblies and weapons. Hire actual local trades to staff those
recipes. You may cancel unfinished paid work before changing recipe; wages stay
spent. Workshop inventory is private, so it can buffer real purchased inputs.

A Friendly shipyard with your operating workshop can quote a five-day funded
drydock expansion. Deliver missing public materials, fund its business purse
and reserve the eight-person construction team. Wages remain held until earned.
Up to two extra chartered slips share the harbor's existing finite construction
workforce. More slips do not supply more workers, materials or free boats.

PEOPLE AND PAID SHIFTS — v0.022
At a harbor, open People, paid shifts & working conditions. Town food security
uses food actually bought; health and unrest explain food and paid-job facts.
These reports add no second universal hunger penalty.

Choose a policy for an owned business while at its home port. Normal work pays
its staff as before. Overtime pays each employee an extra half wage for up to25%
more paid work; inputs still limit output. Extra spending defaults to a5,000-coin
daily cap. Fatigue eventually prevents further rush shifts. Rest retains base
payroll with no output and recovers fatigue. Food relief uses one privately
owned food per employee and10% preparation time. You can set a continuing policy
without renewing it every day.

Three unpaid economic days cause a persistent stoppage with a visible reason.
Fund the operating purse and provide one food per employee plus one repair kit.
Home negotiation pays all arrears and25 clerk/44 registry-foreman coins. An
actual paid public registry must have the available foreman time. Two later
fully paid rest days are required; work resumes on the next eligible day.
Missing funds, food or staff do not erase liabilities or reset a strike.

COASTAL GOODS AND HOUSEHOLD BUYERS — v0.023
Fishery harbors preserve existing food; timber towns finish existing planks;
trade/refinery settlements make coastal lacquer from local forestry inputs;
agricultural villages harvest medicinal plants using actual paid farmers.
All four goods use real trades, inputs and available workers. A small team may
use only the workforce remaining after essential work. Fine timber is a finished
half-bundle: its cost includes half a plank bundle plus carpenter and foreman
work. Open Regional goods & coastal trades to see each bill and actual production.

Households buy food first, then optional products from their remaining savings.
The report shows actual purchases, consumed stock and paid wages. Missing luxury
stock creates no extra hunger/unrest penalty. Full stocks limit further output;
an impoverished buyer cannot guarantee a sale. Civilian cargo uses real hold
space, storage and dated market observations, and can be lost with its carrier.

A solvent harbor may buy a small regional lot and sponsor its sealed freight
alongside your other voyage jobs. Source inventory falls on acceptance; only
actual destination arrival unloads it. Your own trading and the sponsor buy
from the same warehouse. There is no second free inventory.

Older saves receive new zero-valued commodity columns on a copied snapshot.
Existing coins, cargo and regional inventories never refill during migration.

YOUR SHIPPING COMPANIES — v0.024
At a legal harbor, open Shipping companies & route accounts. Select actual spare
boats, a destination, a commodity and operating capital. Keep a seaworthy
flagship and enough traveling hold space for your remaining cargo. Assigned
boats and crew leave your traveling roster; their fitted weapons, armor, engines,
damage and stores stay with the same hulls. One existing sailor serves as master
at175coins/day total:100 ordinary pay plus75 professional premium. No extra
person appears. The ordinary crew savings purse is split proportionally.

Set a maximum buying price, minimum selling price, maximum cargo lot count,
protected operating reserve and route risk allowance. The preview uses actual
water-route distance, fitted speed and current market quotes. Price spread must
cover wages, consumed fuel, repairs and losses; a delivery is not guaranteed
profit. Empty stocks, poor buyers, prices, blockade risk or emergency repairs
can hold a fleet. Its reason appears in the company report.

Companies physically buy, sail, sell and return using the existing fleet
simulation. They pay real wages and repair labor. Crew food is bought from
crew savings. Commercial goods retain their material basis until sold or lost.
Working stores retain their carried basis until consumed; lost hulls charge
the declared standard production value allocated at charter. Those valuations
are accounting records, not extra coin purses. Master/crew wages, material use,
repairs and hull losses reduce the reported net result; capital deposits do not
raise it. Transferred fittings remain physical property and are never reset.

Recall orders a real homeward route. Meet a company at an actual harbor to hand
over capital or settle crew arrears. Collect at its home only when both fleets
are physically there, crew debts are settled and the returned cargo fits. The
same boats and purses transfer once; damage is not healed. A wrecked operation
keeps its finite wrecks and accounts. Government replacement subsidies and
automatic replacement hulls are unavailable to your private company. Actual
recovery and paid owned replacement boats remain your responsibility.

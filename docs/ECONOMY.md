# Bronze cash and the working coast

One sailor-day is 100 bronze cash plus rations. Five sailors cost 500 cash/day. This is the owner's Song-era wage anchor for a fictional motorboat setting. A documented1102–1106 Chinese cash coin is copper alloy; no historical motorboat price is claimed. [British Museum coin](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1979-0305-55).

Each settlement has an aggregate business purse, resident household purse, expert purse and government treasury. Every fleet owner has operating cash and a separate crew purse. The player also has shared sailors' savings, unpaid wage liability and any harbor loan. Accounts represent groups; individual residents/businesses are not animated or separately simulated.

World creation seeds initial cash and physical capital. The complete coin stock is then fixed. Production pays wages to households and professional supervision to experts. Residents buy goods, firms sell and replenish inputs, government collects tax and pays public workers/procurement. Firms can exhaust payroll/stock; ticks do not refill cash. Subsidies and rescue loans debit existing treasuries. Debt/arrears are liabilities, not new spendable money.

Sixteen abstract trading lots form a recursive production graph: logs→planks; ore/charcoal→iron; sealed mining/refining goods→powder; iron/copper/powder→ammunition; metal/fuel/kits plus mechanics/engineers→engines; metal/planks plus gunsmiths/engineers→guns. One ammunition case contains 50 ready rounds. These are economic abstractions, not practical manufacturing recipes. Market cost-trace buttons expose each input and paid occupation.

Ordinary workers earn 100/day; carpenters/smelters/refiners/shipwrights 125; mechanics/gunsmiths/powder workers 150; foremen 175; engineers/master shipwrights 200. Recursive input/labor costs underpin prices; local scarcity, regional specialization and trading spread adjust them. Productivity assumptions are explicit game balance, not historical measurements.

The Tinwake skiff is derived as a 6.20 m × 2.11 m wooden motor craft. The scale reference is FAO's 5.2–8.5 m planked V-bottom fishing boats; armored craft are fictional extrapolations. No plans/art are copied. [FAO reference](https://www.fao.org/4/y5649e/y5649e00.htm).

Its base bill includes one 17,265-cash engine, one 7,809-cash gun, planks, logs, iron, copper, repair fittings and trials fuel. Materials 29,790; assembly 3,264; expert supervision 1,353; overhead 373; margin 2,782; registry tax 1,878. Commission 39,440 cash; ready stock including watch/careening 40,317. Actual local quotes differ. Commission equals 78.88 five-sailor wage-days. Larger dimensions, armor, pumps, machinery and weapons increase material and labor needs.

A commission consumes actual port materials, pays business income/tax, and reserves ordinary/expert wages in escrow. Work releases escrow into resident purses. Both civilians and specialist supervisors must be available; neither can work twice across simultaneous orders. Collection requires a completed fully paid hull. Crew and working stores are purchased separately.

NPC convoys, patrols and raiders have persistent actual boats, a home economy, funded operating cash, crew savings, cargo, payroll, food, fuel and repairs. Sailors leave the same finite civilian workforce. Convoys buy/sell real goods. Missed pay creates arrears, lowers morale and reduces movement/fire rate. Defeated owners' remaining money stays in the ledger; capture transfers only an actual portion to the victor.

Governments/businesses escrow rewards before accepting contracts. Victory releases money once. Salvage pays tug labor/inspection/company fee and yields a damaged uncrewed hull. A failed captain can borrow treasury cash for a real available replacement and stores; an empty yard/exhausted treasury cannot conjure rescue.

The owner's Desktop LTV_Economy mod informed fixed workforce, payroll, stock, trade and fleet operating-cost concepts. Its implementation/assets were not copied. Copied economy dictionaries run on a worker; completed snapshots publish before purchases/arrivals change live purses.
